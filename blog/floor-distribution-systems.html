Underfloor Cable Distribution Boxes for Raised Access Floors in the UAE

Why Open-Plan Offices in the UAE Need a Proper Floor Distribution Strategy

Open-plan offices and trading floors across the UAE increasingly specify raised access flooring, which creates a problem fit-out contractors run into on almost every project: how do you get power, data and AV to a desk cluster in the middle of the floor plate without trailing cables or surface trunking ruining the finish? Underfloor cable distribution boxes solve this by sitting inside the raised floor void itself, delivering outlets flush with the tile line. If you're sourcing floor distribution systems in Ajman, Dubai or anywhere else in the Emirates, the choice of box isn't just about appearance — it's about matching the box depth to your actual floor void and getting the right outlet mix for the fit-out brief.

The Core Issue: Void Depth and Outlet Mismatch

The most common site problem with floor boxes is a mismatch between the box and the void it's dropped into. Raised access floor systems vary widely in height depending on the services running beneath them — cooling, structured cabling trays, power busbars — and a box specified for the wrong depth either sits too high (breaking the flush finish and creating a trip point) or too low (leaving slack cable coiled in a shallow void where it shouldn't be).

The second common issue is outlet mismatch: specifying a 2-gang box where the desk cluster actually needs data, power and AV combined, or installing a brass-finish lid in a space where the design calls for a carpet-flush aesthetic. Both of these are avoidable at the specification stage, but they're expensive to fix once the floor tiles are cut and the box is set.

Matching the Box to the Void and the Fit-Out Brief

Buildora Materials supplies Marshall Tufflex underfloor distribution systems, built to integrate directly with raised access floor tiles for a flush, tidy finish. The range covers floor boxes from 2-gang to 6-gang configurations, so smaller workstation clusters and larger shared-desk zones can both be served from the same product family without switching suppliers mid-project.

Depth coverage runs from 50mm to 150mm void, which covers the typical raised floor heights seen on UAE commercial fit-outs, from shallow retrofit voids to deeper floors carrying heavier cable and service loads. Finish options include brushed steel, brass, and carpet-flush lids, letting MEP contractors and interior fit-out teams match the box to the flooring finish specified by the architect rather than compromising on a single standard lid across the whole floor.

On the connectivity side, the outlets accept power, data and AV combination modules, so a single floor box can serve a full desk cluster's needs — power sockets, network ports and AV connections — through one access point rather than multiple separate boxes competing for the same void space.

Where This Applies on Real Projects

These systems are specified wherever raised access flooring is already part of the build: corporate open-plan offices, trading floors, conference and training rooms with reconfigurable desk layouts, and any space where the architect wants cabling delivered from the floor rather than the ceiling or wall. Because the boxes integrate with the floor tile system itself, they're typically selected at the same stage as the raised floor package, so MEP consultants and fit-out contractors in the UAE should factor void depth and outlet requirements into the floor spec early rather than retrofitting after the tiles are ordered.

For contractors managing mixed-use floors — part open desking, part meeting rooms, part server or comms areas — the ability to mix gang configurations and finishes within the same product range means the floor distribution layout can follow the actual desk plan instead of forcing a one-size box across every zone.

Getting the Spec Right Before You Order

Before placing an order, confirm three things: the actual void depth of the raised floor at the point of installation, the gang configuration needed per desk cluster, and the finish called for in the interior spec. Getting these three details right at quotation stage avoids the rework that comes from a box that doesn't sit flush or doesn't carry the right modules.
