Cable Trays & Ladders in UAE: Sizing, Finish and Selection Guide

Why Tray Selection Goes Wrong on Site

On most MEP packages, cable tray gets specified late and ordered in a hurry. The result is a common and costly mistake: contractors buying the wrong tray type or finish for the environment the containment run actually passes through. A perforated tray installed in a wash-down plant room corrodes faster than it should. A solid-bottom tray used where heat needs to dissipate traps it instead. A ladder tray sized too narrow for future cable additions forces a rip-out six months after handover. None of these are material defects -- they're selection errors that show up only after the ceiling is closed and the snag list arrives.

Matching Tray Type to the Run

Cable trays and ladders in UAE projects generally fall into three functional types, and each solves a different problem. Perforated tray balances cable support with airflow and drainage, making it the default choice for general power and data runs in commercial fit-outs. Ladder tray -- open rungs with no base -- is built for heavier cable loads and longer unsupported spans, typical in industrial plant rooms and riser shafts where bulk feeders run. Solid-bottom tray fully encloses the cable bed, giving physical protection and a cleaner line of segregation where sensitive control or instrumentation cabling needs to be kept apart from power.

Getting this choice right at design stage avoids the two failure modes contractors see most often on UAE sites: under-ventilated trays causing derating issues on loaded power circuits, and under-protected trays leading to cable damage during later trades' work overhead.

Sizing and Finish: The Numbers That Matter

Buildora Materials supplies Caparo and Delta cable trays and ladders across a width range of 50mm to 900mm and a depth range of 25mm to 150mm, so the same product family covers a single-circuit tray in a riser and a multi-tier containment run across a basement car park soffit. Sizing should be driven by actual cable fill plus a margin for future additions -- not just the current drawing count -- since resizing an installed run is far more disruptive than ordering one width up at procurement stage.

Finish is the other variable that determines service life. The range covers pre-galvanized, hot-dip galvanized, and powder-coated options. Hot-dip galvanized is the standard choice for exposed external runs, basements, and coastal-climate UAE sites where humidity and salt exposure accelerate corrosion on lighter coatings. Pre-galvanized suits internal, conditioned-environment runs where budget and lead time matter more than maximum corrosion resistance. Powder-coated finishes are typically specified where the tray is visible and needs to match a fit-out colour scheme, such as exposed-ceiling commercial spaces.

Completing the Containment Run

A tray system is only as good as its accessories. Caparo and Delta ranges include bends, tees and reducers designed to match the parent tray's width, depth and finish, so a containment run can change direction, branch, or step down in size without breaking the mechanical and corrosion protection of the system. Specifying trays and fittings as a matched set -- rather than mixing brands or finishes mid-run -- is what keeps a containment system looking and performing like one continuous installation rather than a series of site-made joints.

This matters most on larger commercial and industrial buildings in the UAE, where a single containment run can cross multiple zones -- plant room, riser, false ceiling, external plant yard -- each with a different corrosion and load profile. Planning the type and finish transitions at design stage, rather than improvising them on site, is what separates a tray system that lasts the building's life from one that needs partial replacement within a few years.
