PVC Conduit & Trunking Systems in the UAE: Sizing, Specs and Where to Use Them

The Problem: Wrong-Grade Conduit Fails Fast in UAE Conditions

Electrical contractors and MEP firms working on UAE sites deal with a specific combination of stresses that lighter-grade conduit and trunking simply aren't built for: sustained high ambient temperatures, direct sun exposure on surface-run sections, salt-laden coastal air, and dust infiltration on half-finished sites. When the wrong PVC conduit and trunking system gets specified or substituted on-site to save a few dirhams, the result shows up later as cracked bends at expansion joints, trunking lids that warp and pop off, or conduit that goes brittle and splits where it's clipped to a hot parapet wall or external wall run.

This is a recurring issue on both concealed (in-slab, in-screed) and surface electrical wiring across villas, commercial fit-outs and industrial sheds in Ajman, Sharjah and across the Emirates. The fix isn't a different installation method -- it's starting with conduit and trunking rated for the duty it's actually going into.

Why Standard Conduit and Trunking Underperform

Most failures trace back to one of three root causes. First, wall thickness: thin-wall conduit flexes and deforms under heat and mechanical load during concrete pours or screed laying, leading to pinched bores that make cable pulling difficult or impossible later. Second, material grade: PVC that isn't formulated to be self-extinguishing or corrosion-resistant degrades faster in exposed, humid or chemically active environments -- a real concern near coastal sites, water treatment areas, or MEP plant rooms. Third, undersized trunking: when power and data cables are crammed into a single compartment sized for less, segregation requirements get compromised and future cable additions become a rip-and-replace job instead of a simple lid-off addition.

None of these are installer errors -- they're specification errors that get locked in before the first cable is ever pulled.

Where Each Size Fits on Site

For MEP contractors, the sizing logic is straightforward once the duty is known. Smaller-bore conduit (16mm–25mm) typically handles single or paired lighting and small power circuits in concealed slab or wall chases. Mid-range conduit (32mm–40mm) is the common choice for distribution sub-mains and risers feeding DB panels. The upper end (50mm) suits larger cable bundles or where multiple circuits are grouped into one containment run.

On the trunking side, smaller single-compartment sizes (16x16mm–50x50mm) are common in domestic and light-commercial surface wiring, while the larger dual-compartment sizes (up to 100x50mm) are specified on commercial and facility-management projects where power and communications/data cabling need to run in parallel but stay physically separated for compliance and future maintenance access.
